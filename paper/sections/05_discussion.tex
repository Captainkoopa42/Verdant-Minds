\section{Discussion}
The observed older$\rightarrow$newer temporal bias in v2 is consistent with a structured growth process rather than unconstrained random linking. One plausible interpretation is that older emergent concepts become anchoring abstractions that serve as parents/scaffolds for later emergents.

This resembles aging effects documented in citation and growing network literature, where temporal position and accumulated relevance jointly shape attachment patterns \cite{papadopoulos2012popularity,wang2013quantifying}.

While the effect is robust under two null families, we frame conclusions conservatively: the data establish a repeatable statistical regularity, not a complete mechanistic proof.
